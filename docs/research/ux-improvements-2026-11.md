# Rebuild HDS around each role's week

HDS 2.0 gets better for its users mostly by reshaping screens it already has, not by adding features. Three moves deal with all six of the owner's notes: give each of the four roles its own navigation, built from one permission map; turn the list screens the owner flagged into interactive, week-shaped views; and replace typed dates with calendar pickers that offer last-day, last-week and last-month presets. Each move matches what the leading shift products (Deputy, When I Work, Connecteam, Planday) and security platforms (TrackTik, Belfry) ship in late 2026. The cause of the worst symptom is visible in the code. The signed-in bar shows My availability, My shifts and My pay to every user, clients included, and hides only the two reports, so a client's first taps end at "You are not allowed to do this." (`EmployeeNav.tsx`; [en.json](../../apps/client/src/locales/en.json)). The next tier makes the manager's work trustworthy: live rule warnings that name the rule and the hours, a publish review, auto-scheduler proposals that can be accepted in parts and explain every empty slot, and qualifications checked against the shift's date. Below that come a client portal built on what the original spec already promises (a billing breakdown and requests), notifications that land in an in-app inbox first, and a design system that targets WCAG 2.2 AA in Hebrew RTL from the first token. The list below has **155 suggestions: 54 Must, 78 Should and 23 Could**. Most are marked **Proposed**, because the original spec describes capabilities, not screens. The research also found pay-correctness risks that need legal review before any screen builds them in. The guards' extension order sets weekly rest from **Friday 16:00 to Sunday 04:00 (36 hours)**, against HDS's default of Friday 18:00 to Saturday 22:00 (28 hours). HDS counts overtime only per shift, while the guards' rules, as Kol Zchut describes them, also count it per week. And Israel's accessibility regulations apply to HDS even though its users are employees and business clients.

## Three owner complaints trace to two design habits

The owner's six notes look like six small fixes, but they come from two habits in the current client. The first is navigation that starts from the staff view and removes a few items, instead of being built for each role. The bar hides "Who worked" and "Hours report" from anyone who is not a manager or admin, and leaves everything else visible to everyone (`EmployeeNav.tsx`). A client therefore sees employee screens they can never use, and the API refuses them with a generic string. Design systems settle this with one test, "Will this user ever be able to use this element?": a permanent no means hide, and a temporary no means explain ([Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/patterns/disabled-states/); [Smashing Magazine](https://smashingmagazine.com/2024/05/hidden-vs-disabled-ux/)). The second habit is screens shaped as lists of records, while users think in weeks of shifts. Availability is a list of "My entries". The work log is a row per person with a first and a last date. My shifts loads only from today forward ([MyShiftsScreen.tsx](../../apps/client/src/screens/my-work/shifts/MyShiftsScreen.tsx)). The market has moved to grid-first and calendar-first views: tap a day or a slot, pick a state, and see the result on the day straight away ([Workfeed](https://help.workfeed.io/en/articles/5081898-submit-your-availability); [When to Work](https://kb.wisc.edu/144002); [When I Work](https://help.wheniwork.com/articles/scheduler-reference-guide-computer)).

The list that follows is grouped by area, as the brief asks. Each row has a **priority**: **Must** means feature 007 should ship it, because it answers an owner note, fixes a role-boundary defect, meets a legal or accessibility floor, or other rows depend on it. **Should** means an established industry standard with clear user value. **Could** means a differentiator or something new in the market. Each row also has a **scope tag**. **Original (ID)** means the capability is in the original product spec, using the requirement IDs and Source column of [hds-spec.md](../hds-spec.md), which is the working proxy for the original; the row only changes how that capability looks or behaves. **Proposed** means it is not in the original. Proposed items need the owner's yes under the working agreement; Proposed does not mean out of scope for 007. **Owner** marks the six owner notes, each named and attributed. Most sources are vendor help centres and design-system guidance. The Deputy and 7shifts help centres blocked direct fetches, so some of their findings come from search-result summaries. Vendor performance figures are marked as vendor claims.

## Navigation and roles: one permission map replaces the dead end

The strongest pattern for a manager who also works shifts is merged navigation, not a mode switch. Gusto makes people with both roles switch profiles from the profile menu, and both profiles must share the same login email ([Gusto](https://support.gusto.com/article/106622133100000/Switch-between-Gusto-profiles-and-accounts)). That fits Gusto's model of separate profiles. HDS has one account per person, in which "a manager inherits everything an employee can do" ([hds-spec.md](../hds-spec.md)), so the Deputy model fits better: personal and team schedules sit in the same Schedule tab ([Deputy](https://www.deputy.com/blog/employee-schedule-view-is-coming)). Clients need the opposite: a separate shell, because in B2B portals "each role finds its own entry point" into the same data ([ServiceTitan](https://www.servicetitan.com/blog/customer-portal-commercial-transparency)). The tab sets in N6 are a design proposal to test with users, not a sourced rule.

## Scheduling: the weekly grid becomes the manager's main screen

The owner's weekly view of shift blocks is the layout every product studied shares: rows of shift types, positions or people, against columns of days. When I Work's "By" switch sets the time span and its "As" switch sets the grouping, by user, position, job site or coverage ([When I Work](https://help.wheniwork.com/articles/scheduler-reference-guide-computer)). Deputy's area view puts **scheduled hours, budgeted hours and actual hours** side by side in the same grid ([Deputy](https://whatsnew.deputy.com/monitor-hours-breakdown-in-schedule-area-view-37pr9u)). That combination is exactly what the owner's view needs: past days show who worked, and future days show who is scheduled. For the owner's half shift or two extra hours, the closest benchmark is Deputy's micro-scheduling, where one shift holds sub-shifts with their own times and the grid draws them ([Deputy](https://deputy.com/features/micro-scheduling)). This exposes a gap in the data model. In HDS a Shift has start and end times, and an Assignment only joins a shift to a person ([hds-spec.md](../hds-spec.md), section 6). One person's half shift inside a shared block has nowhere to live, so the plan stage has to decide how to model it (S3).

Warnings follow two models. Small-business tools warn but never block, and 7shifts allows publishing with warnings still open ([7shifts](https://kb.7shifts.com/hc/en-us/articles/38813443647379-Why-does-7shifts-allow-scheduled-shifts-that-violate-labor-rules-configured-in-our-account)). Enterprise tools separate warnings a scheduler can override from hard stops ([Quinyx](https://quinyx.helpdocs.io/l/en/article/6jiwwx92en-schedule-warnings)). The 2026 direction is explanation: Mesh puts a numbered badge on the shift with a description of each broken rule ([Mesh](https://intercom.help/mesh_ai/en/articles/11689093-new-descriptions-for-broken-rules)), and Belfry shows "exactly who's over their weekly limit and by how much" ([Belfry](https://www.belfrysoftware.com/scheduling)). One constraint from the original affects this whole section. Generating a roster is a manager action, but editing it by hand is admin-only ([hds-spec.md](../hds-spec.md), section 2). The editing rows below therefore apply to admins unless the owner changes that split (decision O9).

## Employee app: availability, shifts and pay organised by week

HDS's four availability kinds map almost exactly onto the four-state grid in When to Work: "Prefer" (HDS open), "Dislike" (close), "Cannot Work" (blocked) and an unmarked default of available ([When to Work](https://kb.wisc.edu/144002)). The leading apps let employees paint those states onto days or slots and see the result straight away ([Workfeed](https://help.workfeed.io/en/articles/5081898-submit-your-availability); [Connecteam](https://help.connecteam.com/en/articles/6569607-allow-users-to-set-their-preferred-working-hours-and-unavailabilities)). Absence is the odd one out. Every vendor models leave, sick time and similar as a separate request with its own approval, and Planday warns that marking yourself unavailable "does not guarantee time off" ([Planday](https://help.planday.com/en/articles/30321-set-your-availability-via-the-planday-app)). Today HDS saves each entry on its own, with no "submitted" state, and absences stay editable after the deadline ([003 spec](../../specs/003-availability/spec.md)), so E2 and E5 are design choices for the owner. For pay, Planday's "Your hours" is the clearest documented pattern. It has an Hours toggle (approved, worked, scheduled) and a Pay toggle (estimated pay before tax, and the change since the last period), and it states that the estimate "is subject to adjustments" ([Planday](https://help.planday.com/en/articles/654310-how-to-enable-pay-transparency-for-employees-in-the-planday-app)).

## Admin screens: tables, forms and reports to B2B standard

Admin and manager screens are mostly about finding, comparing and acting on records. NN/g names four core table tasks: find records that match criteria, compare data, view or edit one row, and act on records ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/data-tables/)). The guidance that matters most for HDS concerns money. Primer says to start every form with explicit saving and never to mix autosave and explicit save in one form ([Primer](https://primer.style/ui-patterns/saving)). NN/g warns that overused confirmations train people to click through: "if you cry wolf too many times, people will stop paying attention" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/confirmation-dialog/)). Baymard found that **31% of sites have no inline validation** ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/inline-form-validation)). So pay rates get an explicit Save and a confirmation that names the change, and everything routine gets undo instead.

## Dates and RTL: calendars with presets, Sunday weeks, isolated numbers

The owner's date note matches NN/g's long-standing guidance. Use a calendar for dates within about a year, always let people type the date as well, mark today, and disable dates that make no sense ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/date-input/)). Preset ranges need a sensible default and should stay in sync with what the calendar highlights; choosing days by hand turns the preset into "Custom" ([Siteimprove](https://fancy.siteimprove.com/lab/components/Actions%20and%20controls/Period%20picker/); [Ambient UI](https://ambient-ui.smartac.com/docs/components/date-range-picker)). No library covers the whole HDS case. The community datetimepicker has no web support and no ranges ([datetimepicker](https://github.com/react-native-datetimepicker/datetimepicker)). react-native-paper-dates has Hebrew built in and says it follows RTL, but it brings in React Native Paper ([paper-dates](https://web-ridge.github.io/react-native-paper-dates/)). react-native-ui-datepicker is unstyled, starts weeks on Sunday and takes a time zone, but documents no RTL support ([ui-datepicker](https://github.com/farhoudshapouran/react-native-ui-datepicker)). Wix's react-native-calendars has RTL bugs that were closed as stale ([GitHub](https://github.com/wix/react-native-calendars/issues?q=is%3Aissue%20RTL)). React Native has no `<bdi>` element, so names, times and amounts inside Hebrew sentences need Unicode isolate characters ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/International/articles/inline-bidi-markup/)). Hermes, the JavaScript engine React Native runs on, supports only part of the `Intl` formatting API and needs polyfills ([FormatJS](https://formatjs.github.io/docs/guides/react-native-hermes)).

## Accessibility and design system: WCAG 2.2 AA tokens before components

WCAG 2.2 added six A and AA criteria that land squarely on HDS. They cover a 24 px minimum target size, a single-pointer alternative to dragging (the roster), focus that sticky bars may not hide, help in a consistent place, no re-entering information already given, and sign-in without memory tests unless paste and password managers work ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/)). Focus Appearance (2.4.13) is AAA, not AA, so it serves as a benchmark rather than a requirement. A vendor-neutral token format reached its first stable version on 28 October 2025 (DTCG 2025.10). HDS can define colours, spacing and type once and generate the theme for whatever library it picks ([W3C DTCG](https://www.w3.org/community/design-tokens/2025/10/28/design-tokens-specification-reaches-first-stable-version/)). The libraries differ most on RTL. NativeWind 4's `rtl:` variants have been reported broken on native since March 2025 ([NativeWind #1433](https://github.com/nativewind/nativewind/issues/1433)). React Native Paper added a `direction` prop only in May 2026 ([Paper #4921](https://github.com/callstack/react-native-paper/pull/4921)). Uniwind, Unistyles, HeroUI Native and Tamagui all merged RTL work during 2026 ([Uniwind #574](https://github.com/uni-stack/uniwind/pull/574); [Unistyles #1356](https://github.com/jpudysz/react-native-unistyles/pull/1356); [HeroUI #459](https://github.com/heroui-inc/heroui-native/pull/459); [Tamagui #4180](https://github.com/tamagui/tamagui/pull/4180)).

## Auto-scheduler: editable proposals that explain every empty slot

HDS's proposal step is already more explicit than most competitors. Sling and Celayix write auto-fill results straight into the draft, and the only way to reject them is undo or "Cancel All" ([Sling](https://support.getsling.com/en/articles/5203721-auto-assign-shifts); [Celayix](https://help.celayix.com/en/article/run-autofill)). The strongest evidence for what to add comes from research rather than vendors. People were **"considerably more likely" to use an imperfect algorithm when they could modify its output**, even when the changes they could make were severely restricted ([Dietvorst et al.](https://marketing.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Dietvorst-Overcoming-Algorithm-Aversion.pdf)). Explanations on their own did not reduce overreliance, except where they made checking easier ([Vasconcelos et al.](https://cicl.stanford.edu/publication/vasconcelos2023explanations/)). That points to editable proposals and reasons built from checkable numbers. No vendor documents a diff between a proposal and the current roster, so AS1 is a differentiator. Natural-language assistants are still early: **Deputy AI is in beta and makes users review proposed changes before confirming them** ([Deputy](https://news.deputy.com/deputy-launches-new-ai-platform-on-aws-to-transform-how-businesses-manage-shift-work-ef9g8q); [Deputy help](https://help.deputy.com/hc/en-au/articles/16154026837007-Deputy-AI-BETA-example-prompts-to-try)).

## Pay and accessibility findings held for legal review

**Flagged for legal review.** These findings affect whether HDS calculates pay correctly, or what the law requires of it. The UI list should not build any of them in until an employment lawyer, and for L13 to L18 an accessibility adviser, has confirmed them. The legal sources are Kol Zchut, a reputable non-profit rights site, and law-firm client updates; the statute and extension-order texts were not read directly. One correction to the underlying research is needed. The research notes say HDS does not model the 175% and 200% rates. In fact feature 005 adds 25 and then 50 points to the weekly-rest rate for overtime worked inside the window: its worked example prices Friday 22:00 to Saturday 06:00 as 7 hours at 150% and 1 hour at 175% ([005 data model](../../specs/005-pay-reports/data-model.md); [005 tasks](../../specs/005-pay-reports/tasks.md)). ## Conclusion

The redesign is less about inventing features than about drawing the data HDS already has in the shapes users think in. The week replaces the record list, the role replaces the shared bar, and the reason replaces the refusal. The research does expose a few model gaps that the 007 plan has to catch early, because screens can't hide them: times for each person inside a shift (S3), expiry dates on qualifications (SI1), an approval state for absences (E5), and a submitted state for availability (E2). HDS can also lead the market rather than follow it in three places no vendor documents: a diff of the proposal against the current roster, a reason and a fix for every unfilled slot, and reminders before the availability deadline. Its hard-constraint solver is a trust advantage worth stating on screen, since the loudest complaint about competitors' auto-fill is that it breaks rules managers believed were enforced.

The legal findings change the design as well as the pay code. If guards, trainers and office staff need different rest windows, premiums and minimum wages, which is what the extension orders imply, then pay settings become a setting for each group of employees, and the roster's warnings and overlays have to know which group each person belongs to. That makes the legal review a dependency of 007's settings and roster screens, not a separate track. The accessibility law points the same way: the closed-service exemption ends the moment one user asks, so WCAG 2.2 AA in Hebrew is cheapest when it lives in the tokens and components from day one.
